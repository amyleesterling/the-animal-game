# Seven story animals: adult size evidence for the field-book scale

Research checked 2026-09-21. All units are metric. **A shoulder-height range is not a ground-to-top-of-head range.** No reviewed source gave a sex-specific *range* for ground-to-top-of-head height attributable to any of the seven **named taxa** at sufficient taxonomic resolution. The honest head-height value for the proposed image scale bar is therefore `unknown` until that measurement is obtained directly for a documented specimen or from a source that defines its endpoint. Ranges below describe source populations or zoo summaries, not universal minimums and maximums for a species.

## Giraffe identity and scale-bar decision

The story currently names a **northern giraffe**, while [Giraffe Conservation Foundation’s Tanzania guide](https://giraffeconservation.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/GCF-Tanzanias-Giraffe-Poster-2022-LR.pdf) explicitly identifies **Masai giraffe (*Giraffa tippelskirchi*) in the Tarangire–Manyara ecosystem**. The [2025 State of Giraffe report](https://giraffeconservation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/State-of-Giraffe-2025-Report-v5.pdf) likewise lists Tanzania with Masai giraffe and northern giraffe recovery in other countries. If the expedition is presented as Tarangire wildlife, change the encounter to Masai giraffe or clearly frame the northern giraffe as a nonlocal guest. Do **not** transfer Nubian/northern size figures to a Masai-labeled animal without rechecking them. The [Tanzania national giraffe plan, p. 8](https://giraffeconservation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Tanzania-National-Giraffe-Conservation-Acton-Plan-2020-2024.pdf) gives adult male and female **shoulder** ranges of 2.4–3.5 m and 2.1–3.0 m, respectively, and approximate overall-height means of 5.2 m and 4.3 m, but it does not define the latter endpoint as top of head.

For the child-facing bar, the defensible immediate choice is a **clearly labeled shoulder-height estimate** with a marker at the model’s shoulder. If the intended display must reach the top of the head, retain `unknown` until a measured specimen and a pose-calibrated, side-on asset are available. A perspective render or generic generated reference image cannot itself validate biological height. Record a measurement endpoint, taxon/subspecies, sex, adult age class, source, population/region, and whether the range is an observed sample range, institutional typical range, or average. Avoid representing a single upper value or mean ± SD as a min–max interval.

## Source trail and review notes

- **Zebra:** Esmaeili, Gogan, Schoenecker & Skoog (2026), [DOI 10.1093/jmammal/gyag044](https://doi.org/10.1093/jmammal/gyag044), Results and Table 1. The paper describes the animal as *E. q. boehmi* (spelled *bohmi* in some result text); the study’s directly recorded mass bounds are preferable for a Tanzania setting to a generic zoo interval. It reports means/SD for sex-specific shoulder height, not shoulder min/max and not head height. The data were gathered 1969–1971, despite the 2026 publication date. A [Saskatoon zoo summary](https://www.saskatoon.ca/parks-recreation-attractions/events-attractions/saskatoon-forestry-farm-park-zoo/zoo-animals/plains-zebra) gives adult male 220–320 kg and female 175–250 kg, illustrating that different summaries/populations cannot be spliced into a single “true” interval.
- **Elephant:** [ADW account](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Loxodonta_africana/), Physical Description, sex-separated weight and shoulder data. The upper elephant head can sit differently from its shoulder, so a shoulder-derived head bar is unreliable.
- **Giraffe:** [Dublin Zoo Nubian giraffe](https://www.dublinzoo.ie/animals/nubian-giraffe/), adult size; [GCF Tanzania national plan](https://giraffeconservation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Tanzania-National-Giraffe-Conservation-Acton-Plan-2020-2024.pdf), morphology and taxonomy; [GCF Tanzania distribution guide](https://giraffeconservation.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/GCF-Tanzanias-Giraffe-Poster-2022-LR.pdf). The older single-species taxonomy and newer four-species treatment coexist in source material. Avoid equating height to horns/ossicones with top-of-head height.
- **Warthog:** [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/warthog) gives matched sex-specific mass and combined-sex shoulder data; [IUCN Wild Pig Specialist Group](https://www.iucn-wpsg.org/copy-of-common-warthog) corroborates mass values. [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Phacochoerus_africanus/) says females are 15–20% lighter and gives a 63.5–85 cm shoulder interval, which is a somewhat different lower bound.
- **Gazelle:** [ADW account](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Eudorcas_thomsonii/) is sex-specific for mass, not shoulder height. The taxon was formerly *Gazella thomsonii*.
- **Cheetah:** [Leibniz IZW research project](https://www.cheetah-research.org/morphology-and-physiology) provides sex-separated intervals but explicitly for Namibia; [EAZA guideline](https://media.eaza.net/uploads/EAZA_Best_Practice_Guidelines_FINAL_SM_f4188181cd.pdf), Table 2, offers cross-check means.
- **Hyena:** [Milwaukee County Zoo](https://milwaukeezoo.org/visit/meet-our-animals/spotted-hyena/) gives sex-separated adult mass in pounds; rounded kilograms here use 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg. [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/spotted-hyena/) provides a combined-sex shoulder interval; [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Crocuta_crocuta/) provides another mass summary. These are not additive endpoints.

No image measurements, game changes, build, browser test, or deployment were performed in this research handoff. Scientific-review signoff remains pending.

## Addendum: one pooled adult interval per story animal

Requested for a simple scale entry. A **pooled mass envelope** takes the lowest and highest endpoint of the documented male and female adult intervals in the table above. It does not mean every mass between those endpoints is equally likely, or that it is a universal biological minimum/maximum. All standing-height cells use the strict endpoint **ground to top of head at rest, excluding ears, horns, and giraffe ossicones**. A source stating only “height,” “shoulder height,” or an upper point is insufficient.

## Further measurement evidence: standing head height and length

Research checked 2026-09-21. **Use the height in this section for a head-height scale bar only when the measurement endpoint is explicitly the top of the head, the taxon matches, and the depicted pose is calibrated.** “Overall height” with an undefined endpoint is a candidate, not a verified top-of-head range. None of the sources defines a standard *at-rest* neck/head pose. For length, the requested endpoint is nose to tail tip, but most zoological accounts instead give **head–body length with the tail separately**. Do not present those as nose-to-tail-tip ranges or simply add the two intervals: tail extension/posture and correlations between endpoints are unknown. All ranges are metres.

**Editorial implication:** For six of seven animals, no reviewed source meets the strict *ground to top of head at rest* requirement; for giraffe, explicit head-top ranges exist only across broader giraffe taxa, not the Northern giraffe specifically. It is more truthful to make the bar an **in-game specimen measurement**: set model scale from a documented size benchmark, show exactly which point is being measured, and label the result an observation/estimate. If using published figures directly, a **shoulder-height bar** is generally better sourced. Sex-specific head-height ranges should stay blank where unavailable. For length, add a visible endpoint label (such as “head–body, tail excluded”) rather than showing a bare number.

**Direct total-length follow-up.** The consolidated [32-species table](ANIMAL_ADULT_SIZE_TABLE.md) now includes sources found after the component audit above: [*Pocket Guide Mammals of Southern Africa*, plains zebra](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781431701315_A20986959/preview-9781431701315_A20986959.pdf) reports **2.30–3.00 m total length**; a [Namibian wild-adult cheetah study, Table 4.2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266454287_Aspects_of_Cheetah_Acinonyx_jubatus_Biology_Ecology_and_Conservation_Strategies_on_Namibian_Farmlands) reports **1.62–2.26 m total length** across females and males; and a [South African wildlife-conflict manual](https://pmfsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Manual_Engels.pdf) reports **1.20–1.80 m total length** for spotted hyena. These source-reported totals supersede the “not established” *total-length* wording for those three rows above; their standing head heights remain unknown.
