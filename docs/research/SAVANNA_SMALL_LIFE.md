# Small animals of African savannas: content research

Reviewed 20 September 2026. This note supports `src/content/savanna-small-life.ts`: five insect species and four rodent species, each with six facts and three questions. Source IDs below are the IDs carried by the content, so a claim can be traced without relying on a generic animal-group page. Text is newly written; no source photographs are bundled by this change.

## Scope and evidence conventions

This is a collection across **African savannas and adjoining dry habitats**, not an inventory of a single Tanzanian park. Range labels are deliberately regional and not exhaustive. Underground animals, nocturnal animals and insects should not all be shown wandering the same open clearing at the same time. A close observation view can reveal a small animal without changing its real-world scale.

Lifespan means different things in different sources. A recorded maximum in human care is not a wild average. An adult insect's remaining life is not its complete life from egg onward. Unknown fields describe gaps in this review, not a claim that nobody has ever studied the topic. Where a gap has a source ID, that source was reviewed for the field; it does **not** assert the absence of all possible evidence.

The questions avoid unsupported ecological guarantees: finding one beetle or termite does not prove an ecosystem is healthy. There are no medical, handling or feeding instructions. Taxonomic aliases help name recognition but do not imply that all beetles, moths, butterflies, termites or mice are the featured species.

## Source and claim notes

### Lamarck’s dung beetle — _Scarabaeus lamarcki_

- [`lamarcki-compass`, University of Würzburg](https://www.biologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/en/aktuelles/detail/news/not-only-the-wind-shows-the-way-1/) supports the South African savanna setting and combined sun/wind navigation. Do not substitute the famous Milky Way result from a different, nocturnal species.
- [`lamarcki-rolling`, experimental gait study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7283283/) supports backwards ball rolling.
- [`lamarcki-breeding`, research chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK200987/) uses the name _Kheper lamarcki_ and supports pairing, burial and maternal attendance. It does not provide a dependable lifespan estimate for the profile.
- [`lamarcki-size`, step-size study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731515/) measures ten beetles: its approximately 2.9 cm mean excludes the head. The profile preserves that measurement definition rather than presenting a universal whole-body length.
- [`lamarcki-taxonomy`, Spanish Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarabaeus_lamarcki) supplements the range. This is a verified exact-species article; an English species article was not available. `wikipediaLanguage: "Spanish"` makes that distinction explicit.

**Gap:** no numeric wild or care longevity is asserted. Do not borrow a generic dung-beetle lifespan.

### Mound-building termite — _Macrotermes bellicosus_

- [`bellicosus-fungus`, BMC study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12862-020-01727-z) supports the fungus garden, castes, underground foraging and Côte d’Ivoire field setting.
- [`bellicosus-longevity`, PNAS field research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6003524/) distinguishes short-lived workers from reproductives. The reported 20-year royal lifespan is explicitly **laboratory** longevity; the paper does not establish that all wild queens reach that age. Its field survival clock after mound emergence is also not age from hatching.
- [`bellicosus-size`, Acta Oecologica experiment](https://www.antwiki.org/wiki/images/4/4f/Schatz,_B.,_Suzzoni_et_al._2001._Selection_and_capture_of_prey_in_the_African_ponerine_ant_Plectroctena_minor.pdf) identifies the tested large soldiers as 17–18 mm. This is a caste- and study-specific measurement.
- [`bellicosus-savanna`, Nigerian field study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/00380768.2011.640922) documents this species in tropical savanna. The [Wikipedia species page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrotermes_bellicosus) is supplied for further reading, not used for its conflicting general size figures.

**Caveat:** “mound-building termite” is not a unique common name. Many different species build African mounds. The modeled individual should be a **wingless soldier**, not the enormous reproductive queen or a winged dispersing adult. No global maximum mound height is asserted.

### Mopane emperor moth — _Gonimbrasia belina_

- [`belina-distribution`, southern African study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615040/) supports the woodland association and short flying adult stage; the paper describes roughly five adult days. The child-facing wording “a few days” avoids false precision.
- [`belina-groups`, Functional Ecology experiment](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1365-2435.1999.00324.x) uses the synonym _Imbrasia belina_. It supports group feeding in young instars, later solitary feeding and multiple host plants.
- [`belina-wikipedia`, supplementary species page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonimbrasia_belina) supports the approximate 12 cm wingspan, eyespots and life-stage sequence. This size is a guide value, not a measured population range.

**Caveats:** model the adult moth; “mopane worm” names its larva. Do not show adults chewing leaves. Whole-life wild/care longevity is not established here. Southern Africa is the profile's focus, not a claim of absolute endemism: a [Kenyan survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8305150/) also reports the species and discusses confusion among similar caterpillars.

### African monarch — _Danaus chrysippus_

- [`chrysippus-zoo`, Welsh Mountain Zoo](https://www.welshmountainzoo.org/zoo-features/african-monarch-danaus-chrysippus/) supports daytime activity and different adult/caterpillar foods.
- [`chrysippus-museum`, Florida Museum](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/exhibits/butterflies/plain-tiger/) gives a 2.0–2.8 inch wingspan, rounded to about 5–7 cm, and broad geographic distribution.
- [`chrysippus-roosting`, Kerala Forest Research Institute report 220](https://docs.kfri.res.in/xxxxKFRI-RR/KFRI-RR220.pdf), page 33, documents group resting in India. The profile retains that locality instead of claiming a particular African social arrangement.
- [`chrysippus-genetics`, East African research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4971206/) mentions a maximum adult life around two months; the zoo page instead gives a much shorter total-life estimate. These are not interchangeable wild averages, so the profile does not offer one universal number.
- [`chrysippus-wikipedia`, supplementary species page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danaus_chrysippus) supports open habitats and color variation.

**Caveat:** this is not the North American monarch, _D. plexippus_. Wing forms vary; avoid heavy black vein markings copied from that species. No North American migration story is transferred to this one.

### Desert locust — _Schistocerca gregaria_

- [`gregaria-faq`, FAO Locust Watch](https://www.fao.org/locust-watch/resources/frequently-asked-questions-%28faqs%29-about-locusts/) supports hopper/adult stages, jumping legs, phase changes and weather-dependent 3–5 month life history. This is a field-biological guide, not a captive-care promise.
- [`gregaria-fao`, FAO species overview](https://www.fao.org/locusts/en/) supports the arid core range, rainfall and vegetation context.
- [`gregaria-chester`, Chester Zoo](https://www.chesterzoo.org/animals/desert-locust) supports crowding, dry-savanna use and sex-dependent **head-to-wing-tip** lengths. The profile's approximate 5–9 cm includes wings; it is not body-only length. The [Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_locust) is the exact species page.

**Caveat:** solitary and gregarious phases differ in behavior and appearance. Use one coherent phase for the model. Outbreak range must not be presented as permanent local residence. No regular schedule for plagues is claimed.

### Cape porcupine — _Hystrix africaeaustralis_

- [`porcupine-adw`, University of Michigan](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Hystrix_africaeaustralis/) supports nocturnal behavior, shelter, diet and variable family grouping.
- [`porcupine-la`, Los Angeles Zoo](https://lazoo.org/explore-your-zoo/our-animals/mammals/cape-porcupine/) supports the quill explanation, central/southern range and distinct wild/care lifespan figures.
- [`porcupine-perth`, Perth Zoo](https://perthzoo.wa.gov.au/animal/cape-porcupine) supplies the 63–81 cm body-length guide. The [Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_porcupine) identifies the exact species.

**Caveats:** estimates differ among sources; displayed values are reported guides, not hard limits. Do not substitute _Hystrix cristata_, a North American tree porcupine, or a hedgehog. Quills cannot be shot.

### South African springhare — _Pedetes capensis_

- [`springhare-assessment`, SANBI/EWT 2016](https://ewt.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/51.-Springhare-Pedetes-capensis_LC.pdf) supplies current species scope, southern range, sandy short-grass habitat, loose foraging associations and burrow occupancy.
- [`springhare-adw`, University of Michigan](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Pedetes_capensis/) supports size, anatomy and night/burrow behavior only. Its old single-species taxonomy and East African range were **not** adopted.
- [`springhare-anage`, AnAge](https://genomics.senescence.info/species/entry.php?species=Pedetes_capensis) reports a 20-year **captivity maximum**, marked small sample and acceptable quality. No wild average is supplied here. The [Wikipedia link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_springhare) is species-specific.

**Caveat:** _P. surdaster_ is the East African springhare. Do not imply that this southern species belongs to every Kenyan or Tanzanian encounter, or that foraging neighbors form a permanent family.

### Striped grass mouse — _Lemniscomys striatus_

- [`grass-mouse-prague`, Prague Zoo](https://www.zoopraha.cz/zvirata-a-expozice/lexikon-zvirat/44-tiskoviny?d=418-mys-paskovana&start=) supports the coat, dimensions and nest location. Czech text was read for this exact species, not its general discussion of mice. Direct fetch can be intermittent; the indexed species text was accessible.
- [`grass-mouse-diet`, Makerere University field study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2028.1975.tb00136.x) supports mixed plant/insect food in wild animals.
- [`grass-mouse-anage`, AnAge](https://genomics.senescence.info/species/entry.php?species=Lemniscomys_striatus) records 4.8 years **in captivity**; this is not a typical wild lifespan.
- [`grass-mouse-wikipedia`, exact species article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typical_striped_grass_mouse) supplements the western/central/eastern distribution and distinction from similar striped mice.

**Gap:** a well-supported wild social-group description was not established from the reviewed primary/zoo sources. “Lives” states that uncertainty rather than borrowing pet-keeping guidance or behavior from _Rhabdomys_, _L. barbarus_ or _L. zebra_. Daily activity descriptions also differ among secondary accounts, so no activity statistic is used.

### Naked mole-rat — _Heterocephalus glaber_

- [`mole-rat-smithsonian`, National Zoo](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/naked-mole-rat) supports the underground setting, dimensions, food, range, incisors and sparse sensory hairs.
- [`mole-rat-biology`, biological review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7149588/) independently supports finding plant food by excavating tunnels.
- [`mole-rat-care`, Smithsonian exhibit account](https://www.si.edu/stories/naked-mole-rat-dream-home) documents animals older than 30 in human care. The profile does not label 30 as the current maximum record.
- [`mole-rat-queen`, Smithsonian research account](https://insider.si.edu/2014/03/secret-formula-feeding-900-babies-scientists-uncover-milk-composition-naked-mole-rat-queens/) supports the queen/colony reproductive structure. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naked_mole-rat) is linked for further reading.

**Caveats:** provide an underground observation or cutaway, not surface grazing. “Nearly hairless” is intentional. No claims of immortality, total blindness, immunity to cancer, or inability to feel pain are made. Wild average longevity remains unspecified.

## Validation performed

Content checks count the nine required IDs, five insects/four rodents, six stats per profile, description length, exactly three questions with three distinct choices, a valid correct choice, and resolvable local source IDs for every referenced claim. TypeScript checking is performed against the shared `SavannaProfile` contract. These checks validate structure; they do not replace the source review or later visual model inspection.

The broader `tests/unit/savanna-profiles.test.ts` checks all 25 new profiles, and `tests/unit/animal-names.test.ts` checks names and aliases alongside the original animals. Together these passed 86 tests on 20 September 2026; `npm run check` also passed. The profile tests run directly on the three content arrays and do not depend on generated models being ready.
